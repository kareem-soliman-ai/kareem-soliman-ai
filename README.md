# Kareem Soliman

Independent researcher working on a recurring problem across statistics, mechanistic interpretability, clinical AI, and evaluation: what looks like noise is often structure we have not learned to read yet.

My background is in psychological science and organisational psychology. I work across measurement error correction, reasoning geometry in transformers, uncertainty-aware inference, psychometric evaluation for AI systems, and multi-agent cognitive architectures.

## Published

- **[The IQ-Motion Confound in Multi-Site Autism fMRI May Be Inflated by Site-Correlated Measurement Uncertainty](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12294)**
  Probability Cloud Regression applied to ABIDE-I. OLS overestimates the IQ-motion confound by 4.67x. n=935 across 19 sites. Endorsed by Russell Poldrack (Stanford). arXiv:2604.12294 [q-bio.QM, stat.AP].

## Research Themes

- **Structured variance as generative information**: treating error, uncertainty, and failure modes as signals rather than discardable noise. 84+ findings across 22+ domains show 30-95% of apparent noise contains recoverable structure.
- **Mechanistic interpretability**: understanding when models are actually reasoning instead of shortcutting. Trajectory geometry reveals explore-then-commit dynamics and dimensional collapse in failure.
- **Measurement and evaluation**: applying psychometrics and measurement theory to AI behaviour and scientific inference.
- **Uncertainty reasoning**: building systems that operate well when the hypothesis space itself is moving.

## Why This Work Connects

Across these projects, the same move keeps showing up: hidden structure lives in the part most workflows flatten away. Measurement error, hidden-state geometry, diagnostic uncertainty, evaluation variance, and relational tension are not nuisances around the real signal. They are often the real signal.

The unified thesis is that structured variance is generative information. Every project tests that claim in a different domain.
